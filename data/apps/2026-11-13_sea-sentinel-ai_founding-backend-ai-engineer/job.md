Sea Sentinel AI · Munich · Full-Time

What we do

Sea Sentinel AI is a risk intelligence platform with a focus on the maritime shipping industry. We monitor ports, vessels, and shipping routes worldwide, detecting disruptions before anyone else does.
When a vessel crashes, we detect it from a local newspaper hours or days before major media picks it up. When a typhoon is still days away from hitting a port, we warn customers before the chaos starts. This way, we enable our customers to be ahead of crises, rather than reacting to them at the last minute. We sell our intelligence primarily via API, which means our customers integrate it directly into their own systems and workflows.
Our rigorous focus has allowed us to build the leading product in this niche. It shows: we consistently win deals with global customers, large and small. We grow because customers pay for what we build.

The role

Our core data infrastructure, PRISM, is built and proven. Every day it analyzes more than 800,000 news articles alongside weather and other data to find the disruptions that matter to our customers.

- 
Owning PRISM. This is your first priority. Keeping PRISM sharp takes two things: solid traditional engineering, and serious prompt tuning backed by evaluations and test harnesses. You'll own this together with the team.
- 
Taking PRISM into new verticals. We're ambitious. Next up are other verticals such as air cargo and energy risk intelligence (LNG, oil, gas). You'll identify the master data each vertical needs, benchmark data providers, and make the case for what to integrate and how to integrate it into PRISM. You'll work with structured and unstructured data and form your own views. This is where the role becomes product work: you care whether what you build solves the right problem.
- 
Working on a system that’s still moving. PRISM runs on messy, real-world data, and new models change what’s possible every few months. You are encouraged to test your ideas against ambiguous inputs and production conditions where things break, and you’ll decide which new model capabilities are worth adopting. Then you’ll prove it with evals.
You won't have a backlog handed to you on Monday morning. You'll be expected to understand the system well enough to know what the most important problems are and go solve them.

What we're looking for

Must haves:

- You've run something in production and dealt with it breaking. You know what silent failure looks like and how to find it, using sensible monitoring and alerting.
- Good coding standards and the judgment to know when to apply them and when to move fast.
- You drive your own work: you identify problems, prioritize, and see them through without someone telling you what's important.
- Analytical curiosity. You're comfortable sitting with ambiguous questions, forming hypotheses, and making a case for your conclusions.
- Real experience tuning LLM systems. You know from experience that a prompt which works well on one model can fall apart on another, or on the same model with slightly different input or settings. You treat model behavior as something to measure, never something to assume.
- An evaluation mindset. Fixing one case often breaks another, so you build eval sets and regression tests, measure before and after, and only ship a change you can show is a net improvement.
- Experience monitoring and tracing LLM pipelines in production. The question you should be able to reason about: if the input changes, or we turn this knob up or down, how does the pipeline behave, and what does that mean for our customers and analysts?
- Experience with AWS, Pulumi or SST.

Bonus points for experience in:

- News data analysis (!), logistics, or energy
- TypeScript
- Web scraping (proxies, crawlers, etc.)
- Structuring or maintaining enterprise-grade APIs
We don't expect you to arrive knowing everything. We do expect you to arrive with strong fundamentals, good instincts, and the drive to figure out what you don't yet know.
We care more about the right person than the right CV. A sharp, motivated junior and a seasoned engineer can both be the right fit for this role. What we're looking for is curiosity, ownership, and the ability to operate with minimal hand-holding. Compensation reflects experience.
